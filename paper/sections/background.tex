\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{Energy-based reasoning.} A reasoner can treat answering as optimization over a latent:
learn $E_\theta(h_x,z)$ so that descending it lands $z$ in a basin decoding to the correct answer.
IREM learns this landscape by iterative energy minimization \citep{irem2022}; IRED replaces the
hand-shaped landscape with an annealed, diffusion-style objective \citep{ired2024}; Energy-Based
Transformers scale the idea and use the scalar terminal energy as confidence and as an adaptive-compute
signal \citep{ebt2025}. Our reasoner follows this template but our object of study is the
\emph{descent trajectory}, not the model.

\paragraph{Distribution-free risk control.} Split-conformal prediction turns exchangeable
nonconformity scores into finite-sample coverage \citep{conformalintro2021}. Conformal Risk Control
extends this to any bounded, monotone risk, choosing a threshold with $\mathbb E[L]\le\alpha$
\citep{crc2022}. Learn-then-Test handles \emph{non}-monotone risks (such as selective error) by
treating each threshold as a hypothesis with a valid $p$-value and controlling family-wise error
\citep{ltt2021}. Selective prediction---answer-or-abstain with a reject option---is classically
learned end-to-end \citep{selectivenet2019}; we instead certify it post-hoc and distribution-free.

\paragraph{Confidence and curvature.} Standard confidence baselines include maximum softmax
probability with temperature scaling \citep{tempscaling2017}, deep ensembles \citep{deepensembles2017},
and MC-dropout \citep{mcdropout2016}; energy has also been used for OOD detection \citep{energyood2020}.
Curvature is linked to calibration and generalization, but over the \emph{loss surface in weight
space} \citep{sam2020,toosharp2026}; ours is curvature of the \emph{energy over the latent, per
input}---a distinct object. Adaptive-compute methods such as PonderNet learn when to stop
\citep{pondernet2021} but without a distribution-free error budget.
