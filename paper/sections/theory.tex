\section{Guarantees}
\label{sec:guarantees}

Both guarantees are distribution-free and marginal over the calibration draw, under a single
assumption.

\begin{assumption}[Exchangeability]
\label{asm:exch}
The calibration and test inputs are exchangeable draws from the deployment distribution.
\end{assumption}

\paragraph{Selective prediction (Learn-then-Test).} For a threshold $\lambda$, let
$R_{\mathrm{sel}}(\lambda)=\Pr(\hat y \ne y \mid s(x)\le\lambda)$ be the risk among answered inputs.
Because $R_{\mathrm{sel}}$ is not monotone in $\lambda$, we treat each candidate as a hypothesis
$H_\lambda\!:\,R_{\mathrm{sel}}(\lambda)>\alpha$, compute a valid Hoeffding--Bentkus $p$-value on the
calibration fold, and apply family-wise error control.

\begin{proposition}[Selective-risk validity]
\label{prop:ltt}
Under Assumption~\ref{asm:exch}, the family-wise-error-controlled procedure returns $\hat\lambda$
with $\Pr\!\big(R_{\mathrm{sel}}(\hat\lambda) \le \alpha\big) \ge 1-\delta$, the probability taken
over the calibration draw \citep{ltt2021}.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Adaptive halting (Conformal Risk Control).} The halting loss
$L(\lambda)=\mathbf 1[\text{early answer}\ne\text{full-budget answer}]\in[0,1]$ is monotone in the
agreement threshold. CRC selects the most compute-saving $\hat\lambda$ satisfying the finite-sample
bound $\tfrac{n}{n+1}\widehat R(\lambda) + \tfrac{1}{n+1} \le \alpha$.

\begin{proposition}[Halting-risk control]
\label{prop:crc}
Under Assumption~\ref{asm:exch} and monotonicity of $L$, the selected $\hat\lambda$ satisfies
$\mathbb E\big[L(\hat\lambda)\big]\le\alpha$ \citep{crc2022}. We verify monotonicity of
the empirical risk curve at run time and report it.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Scope.} Both statements are \emph{marginal} over the calibration draw and hold only under
Assumption~\ref{asm:exch}; neither is conditional on a particular test input. Validity of the score
mapper is irrelevant to \emph{validity} of the certificate (only to its \emph{power}), because the
mapper is fit on a fold disjoint from calibration. We deliberately show in
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} that both guarantees fail under distribution shift, where
Assumption~\ref{asm:exch} is violated---the motivation for treating an abstention as a routing signal
rather than a promise.
