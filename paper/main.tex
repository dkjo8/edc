\title{Energy Descent Certificates: Distribution-Free Selective Prediction and
Adaptive Halting from Inference-Time Landscape Geometry}
\author{Polished Snow}
\date{Draft --- \today}

\begin{abstract}
An energy-based reasoner answers by optimizing a learned energy over a latent---it
\emph{descends} to an answer. We show that the per-input \emph{geometry} of that descent---basin
agreement across stochastic restarts, local curvature of the energy over the latent, and descent
dynamics---is a calibratable predictor of correctness, and that distribution-free risk control turns
it into two certificates: a guaranteed error rate among answered inputs (selective prediction, via
Learn-then-Test) and a guaranteed error budget under early stopping (adaptive halting, via Conformal
Risk Control). On two structurally distinct reasoning tasks, landscape geometry beats the scalar
terminal energy used by Energy-Based Transformers as a nonconformity score (paired-bootstrap
$\Delta$AURC CI excluding zero, $5/5$ seeds each), the advantage is caused by the restart geometry
(it grows with the number of restarts; a single descent barely separates) and driven mainly by basin
agreement, and halting saves ${\sim}58\%$ of inference compute at a guaranteed error budget. Against a stronger, standard softmax-confidence baseline (maximum softmax probability, temperature
scaling, entropy), geometry only \emph{ties} (arithmetic) or \emph{loses} (graph) under a supervised
basin-center reasoner---but when the reasoner is a genuinely learned, multi-basin landscape,
geometry \emph{beats} softmax confidence on arithmetic ($4/5$ seeds; on graph it ties), indicating
that the landscape, not the geometry idea, gates the effect. The certificates are valid in-distribution and, by design, break
under distribution shift, which is the argument for treating an abstention as a routing signal in
critical systems.
\end{abstract}
