\begin{abstract}
An energy-based reasoner answers by optimizing a learned energy over a latent---it
\emph{descends} to an answer. We show that the per-input \emph{geometry} of that descent
---basin agreement across stochastic restarts, local curvature of the energy over the latent,
and descent dynamics---is a calibratable predictor of correctness, and that distribution-free
risk control turns it into a certificate: a guaranteed error rate among answered inputs
(selective prediction) and a guaranteed error budget under early stopping (adaptive halting).
Across reasoning tasks, landscape geometry beats the scalar terminal energy used by
Energy-Based Transformers as a nonconformity score. % (pending experiments)
\end{abstract}
